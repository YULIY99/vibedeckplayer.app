Feature / BPM

See the BPM of every song. And sort your queue by tempo

VibeDeck Player shows the BPM of the track you are playing right on the main screen. In the queue you can sort by BPM: tracks are grouped by tempo range, with the exact BPM on every track. It works offline on iPhone and iPad, with your own MP3, FLAC and WAV files.

DJs, runners and dancers all ask the same question: how fast is this song? Tapping along or opening a separate BPM app takes time. VibeDeck shows the BPM of every track in your library and lets you line them up by tempo, so the next song always fits.

How to find the BPM of a song

  1. Install VibeDeck Player from the App Store and import your music.
  2. Play a track. The BPM appears on the main screen.
  3. Open the queue and sort by BPM. Tracks are grouped by tempo range, with the exact BPM on each one.
  4. Need a different tempo? Open the track in VibeDeck Studio and move the tempo. Key-lock keeps the pitch the same.

Who uses it

  • DJs: line up tracks with close tempos and plan smooth transitions.
  • Producers: check the tempo of a reference track before you start.
  • Runners and gym: build a queue that matches your pace.
  • Dancers: find songs at the right speed for practice, then slow them down if needed.

Good to know

  • Your own files: VibeDeck works with music files stored on your device, such as MP3, FLAC and WAV.
  • Offline: no internet is needed.
  • Loops on the beat: Studio loops from half a bar to 8 bars snap to the tempo grid.

FAQ / 05

BPM: FAQ

How do I find the BPM of a song on iPhone?

Import the song into VibeDeck Player and play it. The BPM is shown on the main screen.

Can I sort my music by BPM?

Yes. Sort the queue by BPM: tracks are grouped by tempo range, with the exact BPM on every track.

Can I change the BPM of a song?

Yes. In VibeDeck Studio, move the tempo from -100% to +100%. Key-lock keeps the pitch the same.

Does it work offline?

Yes. VibeDeck works with the music files on your device, with no internet.

Which files work?

Your own audio files, such as MP3, AAC, M4A, WAV, AIFF and FLAC. Songs inside streaming apps cannot be opened.
